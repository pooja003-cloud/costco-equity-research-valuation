# Costco Wholesale (NASDAQ: COST): A Great Business at a Price the Cash Flows Cannot Reach

Equity research memo | Valuation date: 31 January 2025 | Price: $979.88 | Author: Pooja Master

> Independent academic research and valuation case study; not investment advice.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Conclusion** | Overvalued on fundamentals: base-case DCF **$337**/share (range $263–$400); the price is 2.9x the base case |
| **What the price implies** | 7.0% perpetual growth after FY2029, or a 4.8% cost of capital (vs. 3.0% and 8.4% in the base case) |
| **Why it may persist** | Membership fees (52% of operating income, 92.9% renewal) make earnings unusually durable; investors pay a scarcity premium |

## 1. Business overview

Costco runs 890 membership warehouses (614 in the U.S., 108 in Canada, 168 in other countries) and sells a limited range of goods close to cost. It earns most of its profit from the annual fee. FY2024 revenue was $254.5bn: $249.6bn of net sales and $4.8bn of membership fees. There were 76.2m paid members (136.8m cardholders), and 46% were Executive members paying the double fee. The model is simple. Low prices drive traffic and renewals; renewals fund the low prices.

## 2. Industry and competitive position

Costco competes with Walmart (including Sam's Club), BJ's, Target, Kroger and the dollar stores for the household grocery and consumables budget, a mature, low-margin market. Its edge comes from:

- **Scale in few items.** Fewer than 4,000 SKUs per warehouse (10-K) give each supplier huge volume and Costco strong buying power.
- **Suppliers fund the inventory.** Inventory sells in about 29 days, and suppliers are paid in about 30.
- **Members pay upfront.** Fees are deferred and received ahead of the service.

Together these give **negative working capital** (−4.8% of revenue) and returns on invested capital of **33% in FY2024** (about 30% on average since FY2020). Among peers, only Walmart has comparable scale; BJ's and PriceSmart copy the model at about 8% and 2% of Costco's revenue.

## 4. Forecast assumptions (FY2025–FY2029)

The base case is anchored to management's FY2025 plans (26 net new warehouses, ~$5.0bn capex) and to the Q1 FY2025 trend (net sales +7.5%).

- **Comparable sales:** fade from 5.5% to 4.0%.
- **New warehouses:** add ~1.8–1.9 points of growth a year.
- **Paid members:** growth fades from 7% to 5%.
- **Fee increase:** the September 2024 rise ($60→$65) phases in over FY2025–26.
- **Margins and returns:** gross margin 11.0% and SG&A 9.2% of net sales, held flat; no further leverage is assumed.
- **Other assumptions:** tax 26%; working capital held at −4.8% of revenue.

**Result:** revenue grows 6.6% a year to $349.5bn. Operating margin rises from 3.65% to 3.77%, entirely from the fee increase. EPS reaches $23.13 in FY2029. The three statements are fully linked, and the balance sheet balances in every year (automated tests).

## 5. Valuation

- **Cost of capital (WACC): 8.38%.**
  - Risk-free rate 4.58% (10-year Treasury).
  - Beta 0.89 (60-month regression, Blume-adjusted).
  - Equity risk premium 4.33% (Damodaran, January 2025).
  - Debt is 1.5% of capital.
- **Terminal value.** 3.0% growth, with reinvestment tied to a 25% return on new capital. It is 81% of enterprise value and implies 10.3x FY2029 EBITDA at year-end.
- **DCF result.** Enterprise value $145.3bn plus $4.5bn net cash gives **$337 per share**.
- **Comparable companies.** Peer-median EV/EBITDA (8.8x) implies $243 and peer-median P/E (18.4x) $313. A range anchored on the membership-warehouse peers (Walmart, BJ's, PriceSmart), with Walmart as the ceiling, gives about **$360–$690**. Costco trades at **36.5x LTM EBITDA and 57x earnings**, above even Walmart (20x, 40x).
- **Sensitivity.** Across WACC 7.4–9.4% and growth 2–4% the value ranges from $263 to $495. Bear/base/bull give $263/$337/$400, a probability-weighted **$334**.
- **Reverse DCF.** The price needs 7% growth forever; even 100 more years of 7% yields only ~$780. The implied 4.8% WACC is barely 0.2pp above the 10-year Treasury: the price treats Costco's cash flows as nearly risk-free.

## 6. Catalysts

- **Fee increase flow-through.** The increase shows up as it is recognized through FY2025–26. Expect roughly 10–11% fee growth, against 7.8% in Q1.
- **Monthly sales and quarterly results.** These test the 5–6% comparable-sales run-rate.
- **Capital return.** Cash is building (net cash $4.5bn and rising), so another special dividend is possible.
- **Next fee increase.** Historically every 5.5–7 years, so around FY2030–31.

## 7. Risks to the conclusion (upside)

- **Structurally lower discount rate.** If investors keep treating Costco as a bond-like compounder, the view is wrong: a 6.0% WACC gives ~$596, and 5.5% gives ~$713.
- **Longer growth runway.** International expansion sustaining 8–10% growth for decades.
- **New high-margin income.** Retail media, financial services or faster fee increases.

**Downside risks to the business:**

- consumer slowdown and price competition from Walmart/Sam's and Amazon;
- wage and tariff cost inflation;
- slower member growth as openings cannibalize existing warehouses;
- multiple compression from 57x earnings toward the peer range.

## 8. Conclusion

Costco is an exceptional business, and the numbers show it: 33% ROIC in FY2024, negative working capital and a 93%-renewal fee stream. But at $980 the share price sits far above every fundamental estimate in this study ($216–$690). The base-case DCF is $337, and even the bull case reaches only $400. The premium is a bet that Costco's growth and safety last far longer than any explicit forecast can justify. **Admire the business; the valuation leaves no margin of safety.**

## 9. Model limitations

- **Terminal value dominates.** It is 81% of EV, so results depend heavily on long-run growth and returns.
- **Stub period.** Net debt is dated 24 Nov 2024, and the FY2025 stub ignores seasonality.
- **Beta.** Backward-looking and includes the pandemic years.
- **Comparable companies.** GAAP LTM figures without adjustments; small peer set; Dollar Tree not meaningful.
- **No consensus estimates.** No forward multiples were used.
